Introduction to Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term alterations in global or regional climate patterns, especially an increase in global average temperatures primarily caused by human activities. It includes changes in temperature, rainfall patterns, sea level, extreme weather events, and ecosystem functioning.

Scientific observations show clear signs of climate change, and these include:

- Rising global temperatures (global warming)

- Melting glaciers & ice sheets (Greenland, Antarctica)

- Rising sea levels

- More frequent extreme weather events (heatwaves, hurricanes, floods)

- Shifts in plant & animal habitats


Causes of Climate Change

Many factors contribute to the Climate change. These factors can be categorised into Natural and Human-induced factors.

Natural Causes

i. Volcanic eruptions – release ash & CO₂

ii. Solar variability – changes in solar radiation

iii. Earth’s orbital changes (Milankovitch cycles)

iv. Natural greenhouse effect


Human-Induced (Anthropogenic) Causes

i. Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas)

ii. Industrial processes

iii. Deforestation

iv. Industrial processes

v. Waste mismanagement


Effects of Climate Change

i. Melting glaciers & polar ice

ii. Loss of biodiversity (extinctions)

iii. Reduced agricultural productivity

iv. Heatwaves

v. Heavy rainfall & floods


Solutions to Climate Change

i. Mitigation (Reduce greenhouse gas emissions)

ii. Shift to renewable energy – solar, wind, hydro, geothermal

iii. Reforestation & afforestation

iv. Circular economy – reduce, reuse, recycle


Role of Individuals In Mitigation Climate Change

i. Reduce energy use

ii. Use public transport/bicycles

iii. Plant trees

iv. Minimize waste

v. Choose sustainable products
